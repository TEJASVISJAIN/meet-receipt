The idea

Every meeting gets a receipt. The founder gets the ledger.

A founder of a 15 to 75 person company no longer sits in most of the company's meetings, yet meetings are the largest unmanaged line item in payroll. Calendars show time. Nothing shows money, and nothing shows what came out.

Meet Receipt reads every internal calendar in the Google Workspace, prices each meeting from founder-set hourly rate bands, and sends a visibly named bot into eligible Google Meet calls. After the call it posts a receipt to Slack: the cost, the decisions, the action items with owners, the open questions, speaking share, and a three-tap anonymous poll. On Monday morning the founder gets one message with last week's total, the five most expensive meetings, and the standing series with their annualized run rate.

It never issues a verdict. A model cannot know whether a decision was good or whether a meeting prevented a worse outcome. The product puts cost next to facts and lets people draw the conclusion. That restraint is also what keeps it from becoming a surveillance tool.

Design principles settled in review

  • Rates, not salaries. Role bands with an optional loaded multiplier. Nobody's pay is stored.
  • Founders see breakdowns, everyone else sees totals. Attendees never see per-person cost or named speaking share.
  • Visible bot, no 1:1s, no external calls. Three or more internal people, nobody from outside, not marked private. Anyone can kick it.
  • Anonymous by schema. Poll votes have no person column. Aggregates appear only at three or more responses.
  • Scheduled and actual. The gap between what the calendar said and what the bot saw is a metric of its own.
  • Transcripts expire in 30 days, video is never stored. Derived facts stay.

Architecture

One pipeline, four outside systems, one seam.

Calendar data comes in through a Google service account with domain-wide, read-only delegation, so every employee is covered on day one without connecting anything. Recall.ai runs the bot. Claude extracts the facts. Slack carries every message. Postgres on Vercel holds the ledger. Tests drive the system through the same HTTP routes the outside world hits, against fakes of all four systems.

Cost formula

attendee_cost = minutes ÷ 60 × rate_snapshot × loaded_multiplier
meeting_cost = Σ internal attendees
run_rate = avg cost per instance × instances per year

Scheduled minutes come from the calendar. Actual minutes are the union of a participant's join and leave intervals, including early joins and overrun. With no bot, actual equals scheduled and the receipt says so. Unassigned people get the median band rate, flagged as estimated. The rate is frozen on each row so history never silently changes.

Verified about Recall.ai

  • Anonymous bots wait in the Meet waiting room until a host admits them. The organizer gets a Slack nudge; a timeout falls back to scheduled cost.
  • Participants arrive as display names; emails are behind a feature flag. Matching uses aliases that are remembered after one fix.
  • No built-in consent notice. The bot's name and a chat message on join do that job.
  • Retention defaults to forever for new accounts and must be set. We request transcript and participant events only, never video.

Problem2 / 11

Meetings are the biggest unmanaged line in payroll.

Insight3 / 11

Price is the only feedback loop that changes behavior.

Meeting-cost calculators have existed for a decade and changed nothing, because the number arrives with no context and goes nowhere. The combination that works is: the dollar figure, the artifacts (what was decided and by whom), and the team's own rating, delivered to the person who can act, at the moment they can act.

How it works5 / 11

Read every calendar. Record only what qualifies.

  • Google Workspace delegation reads all internal calendars, read-only. No employee has to connect anything.
  • Every meeting with two or more internal people is priced from role rate bands.
  • A visibly named Recall.ai bot joins Google Meet calls with three or more internal people, nobody external, not private. Anyone can remove it.
  • After the call: attendance from join and leave events, extraction with Claude under a strict schema, speaking share, anonymous poll.
  • Video never stored. Transcripts expire in 30 days. Facts stay.
Unit cost about $1.15 per recorded hour
Why now6 / 11

Bots in meetings became normal. Founders became frugal.

  • AI notetakers normalized a bot in the call. The cultural cost of a recorder is already paid.
  • Meeting-bot infrastructure (Recall.ai) and structured extraction (Claude) make the whole pipeline a few weeks of work.
  • Post-2022 founders run leaner teams and scrutinize every recurring cost. Payroll time in meetings is the last big one without a report.
Infrastructure and culture both arrived
Market7 / 11

Who buys, and what they pay today.

  • Buyer: the founder or COO of a 15 to 75 person company on Google Workspace and Slack.
  • They already pay for a notetaker per seat. None of Fireflies, Otter, tl;dv, Read.ai, Gemini, or Zoom AI shows a dollar cost or a founder rollup.
  • Demand is documented: Shopify cut meeting time 14% with a role-band calculator; Otter and UNC Charlotte put avoidable meeting cost near $25k per employee per year.
  • Adjacent budget: meeting intelligence, calendar optimization, people analytics.

Business model8 / 11

Flat per company. Never per seat.

Per-seat pricing punishes the behavior we want (everyone on the roster). A flat monthly fee by company size keeps the roster complete and the incentive clean.

$199 to $499 / mo

Indicative tiers by headcount band. Gross margin stays above 90% at roughly $1.15 per recorded hour.

Pays for itself if it kills one weekly meeting
Competition9 / 11

Calculators have no outcomes. Notetakers have no cost.

Cost analytics

Flowtrace and Worklytics price meetings from calendar metadata for mid-market and enterprise. No transcripts, no attendance, not built for a founder of 40.

AI notetakers

Fireflies, Otter, tl;dv, Read.ai, Gemini, Zoom AI. Per-seat summaries for attendees. No dollar figure, no founder view.

Calculators and extensions

Fellow, Read.ai, Calendyze, Fire Meetings, a dozen Chrome extensions. One average salary, no rollup, no outcomes.

Our position

Founder-facing ledger: cost + artifacts + team rating, flat price, structural privacy.

Main risk: a notetaker adds a cost column
Risks10 / 11

What could kill it, and what we did about it.

  • Surveillance perception. No verdict, no per-person cost outside founders, no 1:1 recording, structurally anonymous polls, visible bot.
  • Waiting room. Organizer nudge to admit; fallback to scheduled cost.
  • Low defensibility. Win on the founder relationship and the digest, then expand into the founder's operating review.
  • Recording-bot climate. Otter, Fireflies, and Granola are being sued over non-consenting participants; Teams now blocks external bots. No external calls, visible bot, calendar-only mode first.
  • Vitamin, not painkiller. Validate with a 90-day backfill: if the first digest doesn't shock, stop.
Kill criteria defined before building
Plan11 / 11

Two weeks to a real receipt.

  • Week 1: skeleton, roster and bands, calendar ingestion with 90-day backfill, first Monday digest (scheduled costs).
  • Week 2: bot lifecycle, attendance, extraction, receipts, anonymous poll, speaking share.
  • Weeks 3 and 4: daily test meetings on our own team, measure admit rate and name-match accuracy, decide go or no-go.

Success: the founder reads the digest four Mondays in a row and changes at least one standing meeting because of it.

Market check

Is anyone else doing this?

Short answer: the "dollar figure on a calendar event" layer is commoditized. Adjacent notetakers

Fireflies, Otter, tl;dv, Fathom, Granola, Gemini in Meet, Zoom AI Companion, and Microsoft Copilot all produce the "facts" half of a receipt: summaries, action items, and in several cases per-speaker talk time and monologue length (Fireflies, tl;dv). None shows a dollar cost per meeting or a founder-level rollup, per their documentation and 2026 comparison roundups (TechRepublic). Any of them could add a cost column in a sprint. That is the defensibility problem.

Demand evidence

  • Shopify, 2023: targeted 322k meeting hours removed; CFO: "No one would expense a $500 dinner." 14% drop in meeting time per employee.
  • Otter.ai and UNC Charlotte, 2022: roughly $25k per employee per year spent on unnecessary meetings; 5.7 of 13.7 weekly meeting hours avoidable.
  • Atlassian, 2024: meetings miss their goal 60% of the time; the top barrier to getting work done.
  • Microsoft Work Trend Index, 2023: inefficient meetings ranked the number one productivity disruptor.
  • MeetingsCost.com, April 2026: models a 50-person startup at $800k to $1.5M per year in meeting cost. Vendors (Flowtrace, Worklytics) are actively marketing the category in 2025 and 2026.

Risks others have hit

  • Visible cost counters backfire. Experts told Fortune Shopify's tool "makes employees feel like numbers"; one company's meeting clocks were removed within a week. Our visibility rules exist because of this.
  • Individual analytics trigger surveillance backlash. Microsoft Productivity Score was called a surveillance tool; Microsoft removed user names and made metrics org-level only.
  • Recording-bot litigation is active. Otter (consolidated 2025), Fireflies (2025), and Granola (July 2026). Plaintiffs were non-users recorded without consent, which is exactly the external-guest exposure the no-external rule and the unrecognized-participant flag address.
  • Platforms are starting to block bots. Microsoft Teams now flags external bots and added a tenant policy to block them (rollout Aug to Sep 2026). Not Google Meet yet, but a precedent that makes the Recall dependency the riskiest part of the design.
  • Commoditization by Google and Microsoft killed Meetric, Vowel, and Clockwise. The notes layer is not where the value can live.

Where the gap actually is. Flowtrace, Worklytics, and Viva are governance tools priced and positioned for mid-market and enterprise. The small-company options are toys with no rollups. Nobody verified combines attendance-based role-band cost, transcript facts, an anonymous rating, and a founder digest with run rates. That stitching is open. Shopify's never-productized internal tool is the strongest evidence that the role-band approach works when adoption is top-down.

5.5/10

A strong feature and a credible wedge. A risky standalone company.

The problem is real and well evidenced, the build is cheap, and the specific founder-facing combination is open. Against that: every component exists somewhere, a notetaker can add a cost column in a sprint, visible cost figures have a documented morale backlash, and the recording-bot legal climate hardened in 2025 and 2026.

What I would do

  • Ship the calendar-only half first. Slices 01 to 05 and 13 produce the 90-day backfill, the Monday digest with run rates, and the organizer threshold alert with no bot, no transcript, and no recording risk. That alone is more than Fellow, Read.ai, or Calendyze offer a founder. If the first digest does not make you cancel or shrink a meeting, stop there.
  • Add the bot only if the digest earns it. Receipts, extraction, speaking share, and the poll are the differentiator and also the entire legal and cultural exposure. Earn the right to introduce them.
  • Keep the rules that came out of review. No verdict, founder-only breakdowns, no 1:1s, no external calls, anonymity by schema. Shopify and Microsoft both learned the per-person version the hard way.
  • Defensibility has to come from the founder relationship, not the feature. The natural expansion is the founder's weekly operating review: meetings are the first line item, not the last.
